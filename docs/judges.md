# Judges' crib sheet

select count(*) from field_log where at::date = '2026-10-22' and by_profile is not null;      -- fields set or confirmed tonight

**Real venues and data entered on the night.** 153 London venues, every one seeded from evidence, not a map (143 from events that happened there, 10 from upcoming events listed there): 389 past-event records (182 Luma, 177 Meetup, 30 Devpost) from 80 communities, events up to 22 Oct 2026. 66 venues have hosted 2+ events and 24 have hosted 2+ different communities. 64 venues are within 2 km of Old Street; 43 of them hosted something in the last 12 months. 60 upcoming London events (33 Meetup, 24 Luma, 2 BuilderBase, 1 Devpost), 41 linked to a known venue. Leads add more by pasting a Luma link: the venue, its address and their event as a past host come straight from Luma, then 3 questions cover what only organisers know (capacity/AV, terms/evenings, contact/OK to call). Honest gaps: seeded venues have no confirmed capacity or terms until an organiser fills them in, and are ranked below confirmed ones; a few venues are noisy (Adam Smith Institute has 49 records from one think-tank calendar).

**A trust model that stops junk.** Enforced in Postgres (RLS + security-definer functions), so it holds for any client, not just the bot. 1) Root organisers (3–5) can post and vouch. 2) Anyone else can join, but can only post once a member in good standing vouches for them, and only members who may post can vouch. 3) If a venue is reported as junk (one root's report, or two members' reports), it disappears, the poster is banned, and **everyone who vouched for them loses vouching rights**: vouching costs something. 4) Every field records who set it and when (`field_log`); search shows "Fits 120 (confirmed by Lead B, 22 Oct)" or "unconfirmed". 5) Contacts live in a separate table that the public can't read; the API never returns them; only the server uses them to call or email. 6) Call results can't be forged: members can record availability, but only the call pipeline can write `source='call'`. 13 tests in `tests/trust.test.ts` cover these rules (unvouched can't post or vouch or call, no vouching on someone else's behalf, contacts hidden from anon and unvouched, one report isn't enough, two are, a root's report bans and strips the voucher).

**How fast a venue is found.** One chat message: "free space for 80 next Thursday near Old Street?" → one PostGIS query (`search_venues`) filtering by radius, capacity, evening/weekend use and known unavailability, ranked by distance, past hosts, free terms, recent phone checks and capacity fit; widens 2 km → 4 km if fewer than 3 fit. The same call returns clash warnings from the events table (e.g. "Same evening: Is AI making us less creative? (126 going) at Granola"). `search_venues` takes ~18 ms and `event_clashes` ~1 ms on the full data set (local Postgres); the bot's reply and geocoding are the slow part. Today the same task takes days of DMs and cold emails.

**Whether three leads would post their venues tomorrow.** Posting costs 30 seconds (paste a Luma link they already have, answer 3 questions in one message). What they get: their venue's contact is never shown to anyone, they can choose "intro via me" instead of calls, and the venue is called at most once a week no matter how many organisers ask. Their past events are credited as evidence. Name the three leads and their venues here once confirmed: …

**Technical execution.** Node + TypeScript (strict), Postgres + PostGIS on Supabase, one process for API, `.ics` feed and the Twilio media WebSocket; Twilio, xAI realtime voice and Resend over plain `fetch`, no SDKs. Tests run against a real local Postgres + PostGIS (`npm test`): trust 13, sources 20, ranking 8 + 2 label checks, ICS 6, seed 4, demo setup 4 on this branch; the API (17) and calling (23) branches add their own. All times are Europe/London and the code handles the clock change on Sun 25 Oct: "next Thursday 18:30" is 18:30 GMT, tonight's 18:30 was 17:30 UTC.

**Use of community context.** The ranking signals are community signals: which communities have hosted where, how many people turned up (biggest turnout seen), when a venue last hosted, and each community's track record. Venues are found through the events communities ran there. Event ranking follows how builders choose: hackathons and build days > talks > networking, boosted by turnout and host track record, penalising paid events, recurring drinks and recruiter-led events. We hand-labelled all 60 seeded events (`data/labels.json`, good/ok/filler with a reason each; 30 to tune on, 30 held out) and measure it in `tests/rank-labels.test.ts`. Before → after one principled change (classify the event type from its title; penalise paid instead of boosting free, since Meetup never marks events free): "good" events in the top 10: 4 → 8; networking/filler in the top 10: 4 → 0; pairwise order accuracy (share of good-vs-ok-vs-filler pairs in the right order): 0.51 → 0.86, and 0.40 → 0.81 on the 30 held-out events. With the live API's input (track record counted from venue hosts): good in top 10 5 → 7, filler 3 → 0, pairwise 0.56 → 0.87. Caveat: the labels were made by our coding agent from titles and event pages, not yet reviewed by two leads as planned.

**10x over today's tooling.** Today: a WhatsApp "anyone know a space for 80?", a spreadsheet someone stopped updating, cold emails to venues that never reply, and a call you have to make during work hours. Here: one message gets three evidence-backed options with a clash check; one "yes" gets an AI assistant to ring the venue (it says it's an AI, under 2 minutes) and record a dated answer; one more "yes" sends the request with the organiser in Reply-To and CC. The answer is shared: the next organiser asking about that date gets it without another call.

**Real-world usefulness.** It's open source (MIT) and the data is open except contacts. It works where organisers already are (a shared Grok Bot via a public link, plus a subscribable `webcal://` feed for Apple/Google Calendar). Safety rules from the start: never auto-dial (the organiser confirms every call), only bookable venues whose poster said OK, max one call per venue per week, company offices are reached by intros from past hosts, not cold calls. Limits: London only; calling uses a non-UK Twilio number tonight (a UK number needs ~48h regulatory approval); callbacks are post-hackathon.

**Demo quality.** Three real people on stage, a real phone ringing, real data. Every beat has a fallback (see [demo.md](demo.md)): no answer → email request; tunnel or Twilio down → recorded call; Luma down → second link. `scripts/demo-setup.ts` resets the start state in one command (unvouched C, no rehearsal calls), so the call on stage really dials.
